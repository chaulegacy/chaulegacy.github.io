FAQ

Straight answers to your questions

Including the "do I really need this?" ones. No pressure, no fine print games.

"Do I even need this?"

I'm healthy and doing well financially. Why would I need life insurance?

Good question, and the honest answer is that some people don't. Life insurance isn't about whether you can afford a loss. It's about what happens to the people and plans that depend on your income if it suddenly stopped. If a partner, children, a mortgage, or business obligations rely on you, it's worth a look. If nobody depends on your income and your assets already cover everything, you may not need it, and I'll tell you that.

I already have coverage through my job. Isn't that enough?

It might be a good start, but it's worth checking. Employer coverage is often one to two times your salary, which can be far less than a family needs. It usually ends when you leave the company, and it may not follow you if you change jobs or your health changes. A short review shows you exactly what you have and what gaps, if any, exist.

I'd rather invest my money myself. Why pay for insurance?

That's a reasonable view, and many of my clients feel the same way. Term life insurance is designed to be a relatively low-cost way to protect your family while you build wealth. It isn't an either-or decision. Permanent policies, like whole life or IUL, are a different tool with their own costs and tradeoffs, and they are not a replacement for investing. I'll explain both sides, so you can decide what fits your plan.

I'm in my 30s or 40s. Isn't it too early to think about this?

Pricing is based largely on your age and health, so coverage generally costs less the younger and healthier you are. Health can change without warning, and once it does, options can narrow or get more expensive. For many people, the main reason to look now is the chance to lock in coverage while they qualify. Whether it makes sense for you depends on your situation.

I don't have kids. Does life insurance still make sense?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. It can matter if you have a partner who would be affected, a mortgage or co-signed debts, aging parents who depend on you, or you want final expenses covered so no one else has to pay them. If none of that applies, you might not need it right now, and I won't try to sell you something you don't need.

Will you pressure me to buy?

No. I only want clients who see the value and want to be there. You can ask questions, compare options, and walk away at any time. A policy you feel pushed into is one that tends to get canceled, and that doesn't help you or me.

Cost & process

Does it cost anything to talk with you or review my policy?

No. Consultations and policy reviews are free, with no obligation to buy anything.

How are you paid?

If you decide to buy a policy, the insurance company pays me a commission for bringing them your business. It does not add to your premium or reduce the value of your policy. Rates are set by the insurance carriers based on your age, health, and underwriting, not by me. You pay me nothing for the consultation or review.

What if I change my mind after a policy is issued?

Most policies include a "free-look" period after you receive the contract, commonly between 10 and 30 days depending on your state and the policy. If you cancel within that window, you generally get back the premiums you paid. I'll tell you the exact free-look period that applies to your policy before you decide.

How fast is the decision, and do I need a medical exam or blood work?

Many policies offer instant or same-day decisions, and you can often get an answer over the phone. For many standard cases there is no blood work or medical exam. Exact requirements depend on the carrier, your age, your health, and the amount of coverage you request.

Coverage

What's the difference between term, whole life, and IUL?
  • Term: coverage for a set period (like 20 years), usually at the lowest cost. No cash value.
  • Whole life: lifelong coverage with fixed premiums and guaranteed cash value growth.
  • IUL (indexed universal life): permanent coverage with flexible premiums and cash value tied to a market index. Cash value is not guaranteed, and charges apply.
How much coverage do I need?

It depends on your income, debts, mortgage, family, and goals. A common starting point is a multiple of your annual income, plus debts and future costs like college. We'll work through it together so you don't overbuy or underbuy.

What is mortgage protection?

It's life insurance set up with your mortgage in mind. If you pass away, the benefit goes to the beneficiary you choose, who can use it toward the mortgage or other needs. It's not required by your lender, and the benefit is paid to your family, not the bank.

Is an IUL a good investment?

An IUL is life insurance first, not a stock market investment. It can be a useful tool for some people who want permanent coverage and are able to fund it consistently, but there are fees, caps, and no guaranteed returns. If it isn't a good fit, I'll say so.

What if I have health problems?

You still have options. Different carriers accept different health conditions, and I work with many of them, so a "no" from one company isn't the end of the road. Depending on your health, a policy may come with one or more tradeoffs:

  • Lower coverage amounts than someone in excellent health could get.
  • Higher premiums to reflect the added risk.
  • A waiting period (graded benefit), where the full benefit isn't paid for the first couple of years, though premiums paid are typically returned if death occurs in that window.

I'll explain exactly how each option works, what it costs, and what it covers before you decide.

I'm on a fixed income. Will you recommend something I can afford?

Yes. A policy you can't keep paying for doesn't protect anyone. I'd rather recommend a smaller amount you can maintain than a large one that lapses.

Which companies do you work with?

I'm contracted with a large number of top-rated insurance companies, so I can look at national pricing and options side by side instead of offering you just one. Not every product is available in every state or for every health situation.

Applying & reviewing a policy

What information will I need if I decide to apply?

Requesting a consultation only takes basic contact details. If you decide to apply, the carrier will need a few things to process the application, which we complete together by phone or through the carrier's secure application, not on this website:

  • Your Social Security number, to correctly match you in the carrier's system
  • Your consent to a HIPAA authorization, so the carrier can verify health information
  • Your bank routing and account number. Paying by bank draft from a checking or savings account usually opens up more carriers and better rates. Credit cards, debit cards, and Direct Express cards are accepted by fewer carriers, which can limit your options.
What does a policy review look like?

We'll look at your coverage amount, how long it lasts, what it costs, and any limits (like accidental-death-only or a graded benefit period). It helps to have a statement or policy details handy. Please do not send your Social Security number or bank information through this website.

Will you tell me to cancel my current policy?

Only if it's clearly in your best interest, and often it isn't. Before anything changes, we compare the old and new policy side by side. A few important rules:

  • Never cancel an existing policy until the new one is approved and in force.
  • Newer policies can start new waiting periods and may cost more as you age or if your health has changed.
  • If keeping what you have is the better choice, I'll tell you that.
Is my information safe?

I only ask for what's needed to prepare for our conversation and never ask for your Social Security number or banking details through this site. See my Privacy Policy for details.
